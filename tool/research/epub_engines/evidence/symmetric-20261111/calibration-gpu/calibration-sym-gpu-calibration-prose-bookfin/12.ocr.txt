IV

When Jane and Elizabeth were alone,
the former, who had been cautious in
her praise of Mr. Bingley before, ex-
pressed to her sister how very much she
admired him.

“He is just what a young man ought to
be,” said she, “sensible, good humoured,
lively; and I never saw such happy
manners!—so much ease, with such per-
fect good breeding!”

“He is also handsome,” replied
Elizabeth, “which a young man ought
likewise to be, if he possibly can. His

character is thereby complete.”
