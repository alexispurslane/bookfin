IV

When Jane and Elizabeth were alone,
the former, who had been cautious in
her praise of Mr. Bingley before, ex-
pressed to her sister how very much
she admired him.

“He is just what a young man ought to
be,” said she, “sensible, good hu-
moured, lively; and I never saw such
happy manners!?—so much ease, with
such perfect good breeding!”

“He is also handsome,” replied Eliza-
beth, “which a young man ought like-
wise to be, if he possibly can. His char-
acter is thereby complete.”
