# Lay out a hyphenated paragraph with many word joiners in fewer passes

Found in tb-a6zd and tb-i6wg. After tb-a6zd, a hyphenated paragraph lays out in parts of about 256 code units, then whole, and again while a mark changes. Paragraphs with many word joiners still cost 3 to 6 times one layout of the paragraph. On the host (JIT, load 8 to 12): Plato's Dialogues (Standard Ebooks) section 30 item 184 takes 26 ms at 412 x 671 at 20 px and 34 ms at 360 x 640 at 26 px. Darcy's letter (Pride and Prejudice, 36, 19) takes 16 and 15 ms and lays out about 4 times its code units (62,542 at 20 px). The slowest table unit of Plato's Dialogues (item 1292, row 14) is the same cost in a cell: 13 and 16 ms. Each word joiner lays out its part again, and a misjudged hyphen adds a whole layout and parts after it. A misjudged hyphen is a soft hyphen whose line, laid out with the hyphen, breaks elsewhere, so it becomes a word joiner and the marks after it are decided again. A first step is to count which of the two causes the passes. A hyphen width measured with the kerning of its line, or a guess that fails less often, can cut those passes. Hyphen placement must not change unless the old placement breaks the fit rule.

## Acceptance Criteria

- The three units above take at most about twice one layout of the paragraph at both corpus page settings, by the corpus slow-item list or an on-demand benchmark.
- A page dump of the corpus is the same as before, or each changed line is proved wrong before.
