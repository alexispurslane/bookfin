# Show a page of a very large section before the whole section is prepared

Found in boo-rw7u (docs/research/epub-first-page-timing.md). A page of a section waits until the worker prepared the whole section: parse, cascade, styled runs and hyphenation. For Standard Ebooks' Poetry of John Keats, section 2 has 430,000 visible characters and 41,405 flow items. On the emulator its prepare took 328 ms through the page source, so an open at a saved place in it took 1,008 ms (391 ms of it to the shown page), and a scrub into it took 386 ms. A Hamlet act (26,000 to 33,000 characters, dialogue in tables) took 39 to 52 ms. Data parallelism over several worker isolates cannot shorten this, because it is one section. A worker that stays alive is slower here, because its result comes back through a port as a copy: 89 ms for Keats' section, where a worker's exit hands it over without a copy. Options to compare: prepare a section in parts in document order and lay out the page as soon as its part is ready, or keep prepared sections in a store on disk. In the host survey of 10 corpus books, which boo-rw7u chose partly for their large sections, 4 had a section above 100,000 characters (Heart of Darkness, Mrs Dalloway, Ulysses and the Keats poetry).

## Acceptance Criteria

An open and a scrub into Keats' section 2 on the emulator and on the Pixel 8 show their first page in less than half of today's time, with the same pages and saved places.


## Notes

**2026-11-09T22:10:16Z**

Measured before any build (host, flutter test JIT, Mac load 7 to 17, so times vary by up to 2 times between runs).

Where Keats' section 2 prepare goes (warm, about 340 ms in a quiet run): the XHTML parse about 20 to 25 percent (package:xml's tree about 60 percent of it), the cascade about 30 to 35 percent (selector matching about 60 percent of it), box building about 3 percent, flow items about 35 percent (hyphenation about 20 to 25 percent of the whole).

The exact flow item number of a page start needs the styles and boxes of everything before it: a selector such as :last-child reads later siblings, so a part must parse the whole section, and display decides how many flow items each earlier element makes.
